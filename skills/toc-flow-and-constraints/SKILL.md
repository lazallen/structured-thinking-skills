# Flow and Constraints

Help the human answer **What is constraining system flow, and what should change next?**

This skill finds the real constraint in a **recurring** process and focuses improvement there. It does not plan one-off project networks, and it does not build a causal diagnosis tree.

Call the method **Flow and Constraints**. Do not title it Theory of Constraints (the whole body of work), Drum-Buffer-Rope (a factory scheduling recipe: pace the system to the constraint, buffer it, release work to its rhythm), or Ching product names (for example FOCCCUS or “bottleneck guy”).

## Opening

Start with exactly:

> Describe the process you want to look at.

One question. Do not fold the outcome, the constraint, or a guess about what is wrong into the opener. Then work the stages below.

**Always start with Find.** The common failure is jumping straight to improvements on whatever is most visible or most complained about. Name the outcome, **map the flow**, then name the constraint — in that order — before proposing any change.

## Language

- Never call people "resources." Say people, roles, teams, capabilities, and **capacity** (what they bring and when they are available).
- Primary voice: **Find → Optimize → Coordinate / Collaborate / Curate → Upgrade**, then check back. Map to Goldratt’s Identify / Exploit / Subordinate / Elevate / Repeat **only if asked**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Flow** | Work moving end-to-end from a desired outcome to a **delivered and validated** outcome or learning — not merely activity, tickets moved, or releases shipped. |
| **Throughput** | How much **validated value** the system produces. In commercial settings this is often sales minus truly variable cost; in software and knowledge work, validating value is the hard part. More releases, busyness, or utilization is **not** automatically more throughput — when someone offers release frequency as progress, ask what prevents more *valuable* throughput per release. Inventory and operating expense are defined in the facilitation reference, and only when someone asks how to measure. |
| **Constraint (bottleneck)** | The person, team, finite capacity (for example a shared platform), or **market** (not enough demand at the top of the funnel) that most limits overall throughput. Not an abstract problem, a process stage name, or a Jira status. |
| **Queue** | Work waiting for a particular person, team, or capacity. Queues are the easiest evidence of a constraint — but always validate **who or what clears the queue**, not only the visible pile. |
| **Capacity / capability** | What people or finite systems can do and when they are available. Prefer these words over “resource.” |
| **Work as done** | What people actually do to get outcomes through. Distinct from **work as imagined** (how leaders believe it happens) and **work as prescribed** (the formal process). Map work as done. |
| **Find** | Two stages with the map between them: (1) name the desired outcome / validated end-point and org fit; then visualise the flow (steps, who does them, waits, queues); (2) only then identify the constraint from that map. The map is how the constraint gets found, not a later illustration of a constraint already named. |
| **Optimize** | Get more useful output from the **existing** constrained capacity first by changing **how the constraint itself works**: fewer interruptions, less rework and duplicate effort, small process tweaks, tools, or training. Cheapest interventions first. What gets *sent* to the constraint is Curate, not Optimize. |
| **Coordinate** | Change sequencing, handoffs, and early involvement so work reaches the constraint ready and flows through more smoothly. |
| **Collaborate** | Move supporting work away from the constrained specialist to people who can do it adequately. |
| **Curate** | Whoever feeds the bottleneck filters, prioritises, redirects, or says no **before** work reaches it, so scarce capacity handles only the highest-value work. |
| **Upgrade** | Add capacity, people, vendors, tools, or other investment **only after** Optimize and the peer prompts above. |
| **Policy as why** | A rule or practice that explains *why* a capacity constraint exists. Stay in Flow unless the binding why is significantly **outside the group’s control** — then hop to CRT/Cloud. |
| **Check-back** | Agree **when** you will revisit: the constraint moves; continuous improvement needs a date, not a one-off workshop. |

## When to use

**Use** for recurring operational or delivery flow: feature/outcome delivery, deployment, experimentation, support pipelines, and similar repeatable systems.

**Don't use** for one-off project date/dependency networks (Critical Chain), “what is going wrong and why?” causal trees built from many undesirable effects (Current Reality Tree), contested goals needing a success structure of critical success factors (Goal Tree), or enduring both/and tensions with no constraint to elevate (polarity management). Neighbouring methods are glossed in the facilitation hand-offs table.

## Workflow

Ask one focused question at a time. Mirror the outline or map back before extending it. Detail for every step is in [references/facilitation.md](references/facilitation.md); a worked session is in [references/example-outline.md](references/example-outline.md).

1. **Classify.** One-off delivery/project network → suggest Critical Chain. Repeatable process → stay here, at a generalisable level (for example “delivering product outcomes,” not one feature).
2. **Find — outcome.** Name the desired outcome and the point at which it is genuinely delivered and validated. Lightweight fit check: how does this outcome serve the wider organisation, and are we looking at the right system end-to-end?
3. **Map the flow.** Before looking for the constraint, visualise the process: real steps (work as done), who or what does each, doing time versus waiting, queues and delays with reasons, specialist versus interchangeable capacity, variable queues as ranges. One owner’s process, interrogated by the group.
4. **Find — constraint.** From that map, which queue most limits throughput? The constraint is the person, team, finite capacity, or market that clears it — validate the capacity holder, not the ticket status. Then ask **why** that capacity is constrained. If the group already named a constraint, map enough to test the nomination rather than accepting it.
5. **Optimize** the constraint first: change how it works so the same capacity gets more done.
6. **Coordinate / Collaborate / Curate** as peer prompts for how the rest of the system helps the constraint. Order among these three is flexible; all come before Upgrade. If a move is unclear, ask **who makes the change**: the constraint changing its own work is Optimize; others changing what reaches it, when, or in what shape is one of these three.
7. **Upgrade** only when cheaper moves are exhausted.
8. **Close:** ask *when will we check back in on this?* Name which system was in scope if both a value-producing pipeline and an improvement pipeline are in play.

**Proportionate path** when time is short is the same order, thinner: seed a model → place the people → place the queues → which queue limits throughput → what the rest can do. Do not spend the session mapping every activity, and do not name the constraint before the queues are on the model.

## Artefacts

- **Required:** flow outline — real steps (work as done), wait vs doing, people/teams/finite capacities, queues, named constraint, why, improvement moves, check-back time.
- **Optional:** lightweight visual model (incomplete OK if treated as a model, not truth).
- **Optional:** Mermaid diagram — on a **Miro** board via Miro MCP when available, otherwise a local HTML viewer that must show **Diagram rendered**. Conventions: [references/viz.md](references/viz.md). Outline alone is a valid finish.

## Collaboration

Prefer **group validation** of the map (cross-functional) over one person’s mental model. Prefer a shared Miro board for diagrams; otherwise screenshare while interviewing. Treat maps from people distant from delivery as **hypotheses** until doers correct them. Keep sessions lightweight and repeatable — avoid exhausting theatre that people will not revisit for a year.

## Boundaries

- Not Critical Chain project planning.
- Not CRT/Cloud by default — hop only when the binding why is significantly outside the group’s control.
- Not plant DBR scheduling or a full Throughput Accounting course.
- Not Ching product branding as the method title.
- Do not optimize every local step or chase utilization.
- Do not invent the human’s constraint or present a plausible map as verified.
- Related methods stay separate (see facilitation hand-offs). Suggest them; don’t blend them in.
